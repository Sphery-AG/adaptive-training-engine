How the flow is structured

Three Ebenen (levels), grouped into sections in the progress bar — required questions are clearly marked, everything else can be skipped without friction.

1

Ebene 1 — Goal & Focus

Pick exactly one goal (required) — it shapes the entire plan. Then choose up to 2 focus details within it. If the goal is safety- or outcome-critical (Move Pain-Free, Prepare for an Event) at least one focus becomes required too — everything else stays optional.

Mostly optional. Fitness level, current training status (weekly minutes & intensity), available weekdays, and any other sports you already do — with duration & intensity per activity.

UX principles applied

What makes this flow feel simple despite covering 8 goals, 50+ focus areas and a full health/training data model.

One decision per screen

Never mix goal + focus + level on one screen. Each screen asks about one thing, so cognitive load stays low even though the total data model is large.

Section-based progress

15 possible screens are grouped into 3 progress segments (Goals, Setup, Health) instead of 15 dots — the user always knows roughly how far they are without feeling the full size of the form.

Required is visible, not hidden

Required goals/questions carry a small red "required" tag wherever they appear — in the goal grid, the step label and the review screen — so nothing required is a surprise.

Branch only when needed

The health check stays a single Yes/No until the user flags a real issue — the detailed injury screen (pain scale, recovery stage, clearance) only appears when it's actually relevant.

Grouped, editable review

With 15+ possible fields, per-field edit links would be overwhelming — the review screen groups them into 3 tappable sections instead, each jumping back to where it started.

Payoff moment

The flow ends on a generated, visual plan preview (not just a confirmation message) — the effort of answering questions is immediately rewarded.

Reward effort AND consistency

Points are earned two ways — completing a session (task-based) and hitting the weekly minutes goal (volume-based) — so users who show up occasionally and users who train hard both feel recognized.
